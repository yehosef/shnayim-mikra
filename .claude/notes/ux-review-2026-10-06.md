# Shnayim Mikra: UI/UX review and plan (2026-10-06)

## Status (2026-10-07): plan implemented

Every release below is implemented on branch `firebase-sync` and bumped to 1.4.0 (see
`CHANGELOG.md`); not yet deployed. The "Not planned" items remain open. The rest of this note is
the review as written on 2026-10-06.

## Where things stand

On 2026-10-06 the app (version 1.3.0, the release that redesigned Settings) was reviewed on six screen sizes: a 390px phone, a 360x640 small phone, a phone in landscape, a portrait tablet, a landscape tablet and a 1440px desktop. Both interface languages were covered, along with the list view, focus mode, Settings and the focus-mode help panel. The review started from 100 baseline screenshots plus about 100 extra captures taken by nine separate reviewers, and a second pass re-checked every finding against the screenshots and the code.

The core reading loop works well: tap a text, it turns green, and the pointer moves on. The problems sit around that loop, in what hides the text, what crowds the header, and what a new reader is never told. Three things matter most:

1. **One critical bug in focus mode.** When Rashi or English is shown, or the pasuk is long, the pasuk opens scrolled above the top of the screen, where the reader cannot scroll to it. On a phone this happens at the default text size with Rashi on. Related high-severity bugs: at large text sizes the focus header pushes the exit button off a phone screen (and phones have no Escape key), and in phone landscape the list header covers all of the text.
2. **The header is crowded, and you already named the right fixes.** All four of your header observations hold up. The "Suggested today" line repeats the chip, and in Hebrew "מומלץ להיום: שלישי" reads as "Tuesday". The "Last week" link is an unstyled browser-blue link that grows with the reading-text size, which is why it is bigger than the title. The title should be the parsha picker. A brand-new visitor on Sunday to Tuesday also lands on last week's parsha and is told to "catch up".
3. **Sign-in is invisible, and nothing warns that marks live only in this browser.** I agree with your observation. The fix is a one-time welcome card, a small sign-in indicator next to the gear, and plainer copy in Settings.

What you need to do: answer the decisions near the end of this note. The first two releases (focus-mode and scrolling fixes) need no decisions and can start now.

## What already works (keep it)

- **Marking:** tapping a text gives immediate feedback (green fill, pointer moves) without the page jumping. A 180 ms hold plus an input guard stops a double tap from marking an unseen piece, Space never un-marks, and dragging to select text never counts as a tap.
- **Read state:** read pieces are marked only by border and background, never dimmed or struck through. Read Hebrew stays at 13:1 contrast or better.
- **Sizes:** the Hebrew is larger than the targum at every text size, and the Settings size preview uses the same multipliers as the list view.
- **Layout:** no horizontal overflow on any of the 100 baseline captures, in either language.
- **Direction:** "next is on the left" is consistent across arrows, keys, tooltips and slide direction in both languages. Hebrew mirroring is correct throughout: Settings uses logical CSS, bidirectional strings are isolated, and verse cards are pinned right-to-left.
- **Fonts:** SBL Hebrew renders trop and nikud cleanly, and the Rashi-script font covers the whole Rashi data set.
- **Settings:** it is a real dialog, with a pinned header, a focus trap, 44px segmented controls that work as radio groups, disabled options that say why, two-step clear with Undo, and "Bring back cleared marks".
- **First run:** the first-run schedule question is inline and never blocks the page. Keep that pattern for any new welcome card.
- **Graceful failures:** a bad parsha link falls back quietly to the default week. A double parsha (Vayakhel-Pekudei) works as one reading. The yearly archive can be undone. The aliyah-boundary failure path already shows a plain sentence and a Retry button.
- **Reduced motion and zoom:** reduced motion is handled at the core (slides become crossfades, the pulse stops), and pinch-zoom is never blocked.

Severity labels: 🚨 critical (blocks or misleads reading, or loses trust) · ⚠️ high (hurts the core reading flow or a whole device class) · 📝 medium (quality) · 🧹 low (cleanup). Findings are numbered so the plan can refer to them. Screenshot names refer to the capture folder described in the last section.

### Focus mode

**1. 🚨 With Rashi or English shown, or a long pasuk, focus mode opens with the pasuk scrolled out of reach above the screen.**
The content area centres its contents vertically and also scrolls. When the contents are taller than the screen, the top half overflows above the scroll start, where no scrolling reaches. On a 390x844 phone at the default text size with Rashi and English on, Bereshit 1:1 opens in the middle of Rashi's commentary. The "First reading" label and the Hebrew card are nowhere on screen (card top measured at -684px). A long pasuk at text size 24 on a 360px phone shows only the card's last line. The reader is told "Tap to continue" for text they cannot see, so they either mark unread text or give up on focus mode.
Evidence: edge-states-default-rashi-focus.png, edge-states-longverse-focus.png, edge-states-haazinu-focus-he.png. The cause is `.focus-content` in `src/components/FocusMode.vue`, which combines `overflow-y: auto` with `justify-content: center`.
Fix: use "safe" centring with auto margins on the first and last child (or `justify-content: safe center`), so short pesukim stay centred and tall content starts at the top. Auto margins are the more compatible choice, since older iOS Safari lacks `safe`.

**2. ⚠️ At large text sizes the focus header pushes the exit and settings buttons off a phone screen.**
The focus header (aliyah label, "Perek א : Pasuk יג", ?, gear, ✕) is sized in em, so it grows with the reading-text setting. At size 32 on a 390px phone, both the gear and the ✕ are off-screen. At size 24 on a 360px phone the ✕ is cut off and "Pasuk" runs under the "?". A phone has no Escape key, so a reader who enlarged the text for comfort, often an older reader, cannot leave focus mode or reach Settings to shrink it again. At 360px the header also stacks to three rows even at the default size.
Evidence: en-phone-focus-font32.png and its .json (settings and exit buttons in the off-screen list), edge-states-longverse-focus.png, en-phoneSmall-focus.png. Header styles in `src/components/FocusMode.vue` (`.focus-header`, `.aliya-label`, `.perek`).
Fix: size the header in rem so only the reading text follows the setting, let the header wrap, and keep the button group from shrinking. Below 400px, show the compact reference "א:יג".

**3. ⚠️ On portrait tablets (601 to about 1000px wide), the focus side buttons sit on top of the reading card.**
The side buttons are fixed 16px from the screen edges. The rule that pads the content clear of them exists only below 600px. At 820px the green "next" button covers the card's edge, and the faded disabled "previous" button sits on the gold pointer stripe. With Rashi shown, the buttons land beside Rashi lines that run underneath them.
Evidence: tablet-desktop-tab-focus-long.png, tablet-desktop-tab-focus-b111.png, he-tablet-focus.png. In `src/components/FocusMode.vue`, `.nav-btn` is `position: fixed` and the side padding sits only in the 600px media block.
Fix: reserve the buttons' width in the content padding at every width under about 1000px, and cap the Rashi and English block to the same width. The buttons stay at mid-edge, as the binding requirement says.

**4. 📝 The keyboard help panel is cut off on short screens and cannot scroll.**
On a landscape phone and a landscape tablet the panel is taller than the screen: its title and its Close button are off-screen, and only Escape closes it. On a 1440x900 desktop it touches both edges.
Evidence: en-phoneLand-help.png, he-tabletLand-help.png, en-desktop-help.png. `.help-panel` in `FocusMode.vue` has no max-height.
Fix: add `max-height: calc(100dvh - 2rem)` and `overflow-y: auto`, and keep the Close button sticky at the bottom of the panel.

**5. 📝 On phones, focus mode talks about keys the phone does not have, in hard-to-read grey.**
The only instruction under the card is "Tap or press [Space] to continue" (Hebrew "לחץ או [Space] להמשיך"), in #9ca3af on the #f5f5f5 page, about 2.3:1 contrast. The "?" opens "Keyboard shortcuts": 13 rows of Space, Enter, arrows, M, U and Esc, with Space and Enter listed as separate rows for the same action. Nothing explains the touch gestures.
Evidence: en-phone-focus.png, he-phone-focus.png, en-phone-help.png. Hint strings and help overlay in `FocusMode.vue`; the hint colour is `--c-faint`.
Fix: on touch devices show "Tap the text to continue" / "הקישו על הטקסט להמשך" in a darker grey (`--c-text-2`). Make "?" a "How it works" sheet with touch first (tap the text, the side buttons, the reading dots), a colour key, and the keyboard table only when a keyboard is in use, with the Space and Enter rows merged.

**6. 📝 The three reading dots are tiny, unlabelled, and use the aliyah-blue colour for "current".**
The dots (first reading, second reading, translation) are 16-18px plain spans with click handlers: no button role and not reachable with Tab. Their meaning exists only in a hover tooltip. The current dot is blue, the colour reserved for "aliyah in scope". Inactive dots are #9ca3af on #e5e7eb, about 2:1.
Evidence: en-phone-focus.png and its .json (dots listed as small targets), he-phone-focus.png. `FocusMode.vue` step-indicator markup and CSS.
Fix: make them buttons with at least a 32-44px hit area and short labels ("1 · 2 · ת"), mark the current one with the pointer treatment, and draw inactive ones as outlined rings.

**7. 📝 Focus mode shows the pasuk smaller than the list view on phones, and no larger on desktop.**
A rule for screens under 768px multiplies the Hebrew and the counted translation by 0.8, so phone focus mode shows the Hebrew at 24px against the list's 30px. The reference English (19px) then ends up larger than the counted Onkelos (18.4px). On a 1440px desktop a short pasuk is a 360px strip in the middle of a large grey stage.
Evidence: en-phone-focus-rashi-english.png against en-phone-list.png, en-desktop-focus.png. The 768px block in `FocusMode.vue`.
Fix: delete the two 0.8 rules. Optionally scale up about 1.25x at 1024px and above, keeping the pasuk larger than the targum.

**8. 📝 Focus mode has no undo for a mistaken tap on a phone.**
A tap always marks and advances. Undo exists only as the U key. A stray tap records a reading that did not happen, and fixing it means leaving focus mode and tapping the green box in the list.
Evidence: en-phone-help.png. `advanceStep` and `undoLastAction` in `FocusMode.vue`.
Fix: show a brief "Marked · Undo" bar after each focus-mode mark.

**9. 📝 Finishing the last pasuk of a parsha gives no acknowledgement.**
After the final Space, focus mode closes silently and drops the reader into the list. The only signs of completion are a 100% bar and green chips. For a weekly obligation, this is the moment the reader most wants confirmation and a next step.
Evidence: edge-states-last-focus-s3.png, edge-states-complete-list.png. `advanceStep` in `FocusMode.vue` emits exit.
Fix: show a completion card ("Parshat X complete: both readings and the translation") with [Coming week] and [Back to list].

**10. 📝 The chosen translation is silently replaced by Onkelos when Rashi or English is missing.**
With Rashi counted, Devarim 8:9 (no Rashi) shows a step labelled "Targum Onkelos" with no reason given. The same happens for English when the English file is not available offline. The fallback is right, but the reader cannot tell a fallback from a bug.
Evidence: edge-states-rashi-gap-focus.png, edge-states-english-missing-focus.png. `targumLayer` and `stepLabel` in `FocusMode.vue`.
Fix: add one line under the label, "No Rashi on this pasuk: Onkelos instead". Which field gets marked does not change.

**11. 🧹 In Hebrew, the focus-header ▶ pointer points off-screen.**
It is a fixed ▶ glyph at the start of the label. In Hebrew that puts it at the far right, pointing away from what it marks.
Evidence: he-phone-focus.png. `FocusMode.vue` pointer mark.
Fix: mirror the glyph under right-to-left.

**14. 📝 "Suggested today" repeats the chip, reads as a weekday in Hebrew, and shows even on finished or distant parshiyot.** (Your observation 1.)

**15. 📝 The "Last week" link is browser-blue, underlined, and larger than the title.** (Your observation 2.)
It inherits the reading-text size (20px default, 32px at maximum) and the browser's #0000EE blue, against an 18.4px title on phones. **16. 📝 The parsha name appears twice in the top row, and the dropdown's legend is hover-only.** (Your observation 3.)
"פרשת בראשית" (title) sits next to a 147px select reading "◐ בראשית". On tablets and desktop the gear and dropdown form a separate column, so the seven chips wrap onto two rows (header 265-281px on a portrait tablet), and the progress bar ends about 200px short of the card.

**17. 📝 Aliyah chips 5-7 are hidden off the edge with no cue, and chips are only 23px tall.**
On a 390px phone the row shows ראשון to רביעי and ends cleanly; the scrollbar is hidden. A reader can conclude the parsha has four aliyot. In aliyah view with שביעי selected, that chip stays scrolled out of sight.

### Onboarding and sign-in

**22. ⚠️ A brand-new visitor on Sunday to Tuesday lands on last week's parsha and is told to catch up.**
With no marks at all, the app opens on פרשת וזאת הברכה with "Today: review / catch up" and a "last week" pill, while the coming week appears only as a link. The catch-up window treats "no marks" as "unfinished". This week the parsha is Vezot Haberachah, which is not even a regular Shabbat reading.
Evidence: en-phone-list-firstrun.png, ia-he-phone-firstrun.png. `resolveDefaultWeek` in `src/composables/useParsha.js` and `isRouteDone` in `src/App.vue`.
Fix: have App pass a "started and not finished" test, so a parsha with zero marks counts as done for the catch-up window. Add a case to `tests/default-week.test.js`.

**23. 📝 The interface is English for every first-time visitor.**
The interface language defaults to English and nothing reads the browser's language, so a Hebrew-speaking reader in Israel gets English menus around Hebrew text.
Evidence: `src/composables/useSettings.js` (default), and the pre-mount direction script in `index.html`.
Fix: when no settings are saved, default to Hebrew if `navigator.language` starts with "he", in both places so the direction does not flash.

**24. ⚠️ Google sign-in is buried, and nothing says marks otherwise live only in this browser.** (Your observation 4.)
Account is the fourth Settings section, after about 1.5 screens of scrolling on a phone, and it is also below the fold on desktop and tablet. Nothing on the main screen mentions it. The section opens with the data disclosure ("Stores your Google name and email…") before the benefit, and the helper ends "Everything works without it." Without sign-in, marks are lost when site data is cleared, on a new phone, or after Safari's storage purge for a site not added to the Home Screen.
Evidence: phen-settings-account.png, rtl-he-phone-settings-account.png, en-phone-settings.png, en-phone-list-firstrun.png. Account section and copy in `src/components/SettingsModal.vue`; first-run banner in `App.vue`.

The copy leads with the benefit, then says plainly "Without signing in, your marks are kept only in this browser on this device", then makes the disclosure conditional ("If you sign in, we store…").

**25. 📝 The "app has moved" notice says to sign in "here (Settings)" but offers no button.**
Readers on the old address must find the gear and scroll to Account. Those who give up arrive at the new address with no marks, which is the loss the notice exists to prevent.
Evidence: the moved notice in `App.vue`.

**28. 📝 Focus mode is reachable only through an unlabelled 🔍, which reads as "search".**

### Reading and marking in the list view

**29. ⚠️ On a phone, the gold "you are here" pointer is a half-clipped triangle, and purple does its job.**
The purple keyboard-selection style is declared after the gold pointer style and always wins, so the current card's edge and box are purple. The only gold left is a ▶ placed 12px outside the card, which the 8px phone gutter cuts in half. Focus mode uses gold for the same meaning, so the two views disagree.
Evidence: en-phone-list.png, interaction-unmark.png. `.verse-pointer` (`left: -12px`) and the order of `.verse.selected` after `.current-verse` in `VerseView.vue`.
Fix: move the ▶ inside the card on the reading-start side. Draw the pointer gold, and show purple only when the keyboard has moved the selection elsewhere (finding 31).

**30. 📝 The pointer and several hints are too faint.**
The pointer colour #d4a574 is about 2:1 on the page, below the 3:1 minimum for meaningful marks, and its pulse dips to 60% opacity. The focus-mode hint and separators use #9ca3af (2.3:1).
Evidence: en-phone-list.png, en-phone-focus-rashi-english.png. `--c-pointer` and `--c-faint` in `src/style.css`; the pulse keyframes in `VerseView.vue`.
Fix: add a stronger tan (about #8b5e34) for ▶ glyphs only, keeping the current tan for rings and stripes. Pulse by movement only, never opacity. Use `--c-text-2` for text the reader must read.

**31. 📝 The purple keyboard selection is always shown on touch phones.**
It means "the piece the next Space press marks". A phone has no Space key, yet purple is the strongest colour on screen, and it jumps to whatever was last tapped, including an un-marked piece.
Evidence: en-phone-list.png, interaction-unmark.png. `.phase-selected` and `.verse.selected` in `VerseView.vue`.
Fix (needs your decision): a root flag set on keydown and cleared on pointerdown, with the purple styles scoped under it. This changes when the existing classes apply, not how they look.

**32. ⚠️ After advancing, the pasuk number and the top lines of a long pasuk hide under the sticky arrow row.**
Advancing scrolls the selected piece to the centre of the whole window and ignores the sticky header. In one-pasuk mode the card's top row (pasuk number, 🔍, the completion dot, the pointer) ends up under the arrow row. A long pasuk (Bamidbar 8:19 at size 24 on 360x640) opens mid-verse.
